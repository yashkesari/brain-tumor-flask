Brain Tumor Awareness & Education

Learn about brain tumor types, symptoms, diagnostic methods, and treatment approaches. This information is compiled from publicly available medical literature for educational purposes.

What Are Brain Tumors?

Overview and classification

A brain tumor is an abnormal growth of cells within the brain or the central spinal canal. Brain tumors can be primary (originating in the brain) or secondary/metastatic (spreading from other parts of the body).

Primary brain tumors are classified as either benign (non-cancerous, slower-growing) or malignant (cancerous, faster-growing). The World Health Organization (WHO) grades brain tumors from Grade I (least aggressive) to Grade IV (most aggressive).

Common Brain Tumor Types

The types detected by NeuroScan AI's classifier

Glioma

WHO Grade I–IV

Gliomas arise from glial cells — the supportive cells of the nervous system. They are the most common type of primary brain tumor, accounting for about 33% of all brain tumors.

Subtypes Astrocytoma, Oligodendroglioma, Glioblastoma (GBM)
Glioblastoma Most aggressive form (Grade IV). Median survival 14–16 months with treatment.
Common location Cerebral hemispheres, often frontal and temporal lobes

Meningioma

WHO Grade I–III (mostly Grade I)

Meningiomas develop from the meninges — the protective membranes surrounding the brain and spinal cord. They are the most common benign brain tumor, representing about 37% of all primary brain tumors.

Behavior Most are slow-growing and benign. About 2% are malignant.
Treatment Often curable with surgical removal. Some small ones are monitored.
Common location Along the surface of the brain (convexity, parasagittal, skull base)

Pituitary Tumor

Usually benign (adenoma)

Pituitary tumors develop in the pituitary gland at the base of the brain. This pea-sized gland controls many hormones. Pituitary adenomas account for about 16% of primary brain tumors.

Types Functioning (hormone-producing) and non-functioning
Symptoms Vision changes, hormonal imbalances, headaches
Prognosis Generally excellent. Most are treatable with surgery or medication.

Normal Brain Tissue

No tumor detected

Warning Signs & Symptoms

When to seek medical attention

Brain tumor symptoms depend on the tumor's size, location, and growth rate. Some tumors may not cause symptoms until they become large. If you experience any combination of the following symptoms persistently, consult a healthcare professional.

🤕
Persistent Headaches
New or worsening headaches, especially in the morning or with changes in position
⚡
Seizures
New-onset seizures in someone without a seizure history
👁️
Vision Changes
Blurred or double vision, loss of peripheral vision
💪
Weakness or Numbness
Progressive weakness or numbness in arms or legs, especially on one side
🗣️
Speech Difficulties
Difficulty speaking, understanding language, or finding words
🧠
Cognitive Changes
Memory problems, confusion, personality changes, difficulty concentrating
🤢
Nausea & Vomiting
Unexplained nausea, especially in the morning, may indicate increased intracranial pressure
⚖️
Balance Problems
Difficulty walking, dizziness, loss of coordination

Diagnosis & Imaging

How brain tumors are detected

🔬 MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging)

The gold standard for brain tumor detection. Uses magnetic fields and radio waves to create detailed images of brain structures. MRI can show tumor size, location, and sometimes composition. Contrast-enhanced MRI (with gadolinium) helps distinguish tumor tissue from normal tissue.

🖥️ CT Scan

Uses X-rays to create cross-sectional images. Often the first imaging test performed in emergency settings. Faster than MRI but provides less detail for soft tissue. Useful for detecting calcifications and acute bleeding.

🧬 Biopsy

A tissue sample is taken from the tumor for microscopic analysis. This is the definitive diagnostic method that confirms tumor type, grade, and molecular markers. Can be done during surgery or with a stereotactic needle biopsy.

🤖 AI-Assisted Analysis

Emerging tools like NeuroScan AI use deep learning to assist in preliminary MRI analysis. These AI systems can identify patterns consistent with specific tumor types, but they are decision-support tools — not replacements for expert diagnosis.

Treatment Approaches

Common treatment strategies

Treatment depends on tumor type, size, location, grade, and the patient's overall health. A multidisciplinary team typically develops the treatment plan.

🏥

Surgery

The primary treatment when the tumor is accessible. Aims for maximum safe resection — removing as much tumor as possible while preserving brain function. Advanced techniques include awake craniotomy and image-guided surgery.

☢️

Radiation Therapy

Uses targeted high-energy beams to kill tumor cells. Methods include external beam radiation, stereotactic radiosurgery (Gamma Knife, CyberKnife), and proton beam therapy. Often used after surgery or for inoperable tumors.

💊

Chemotherapy

Drugs that kill rapidly dividing cells. Temozolomide (TMZ) is the standard chemotherapy for glioblastoma. Some drugs are taken orally, others intravenously. The blood-brain barrier can limit drug effectiveness.

🔍

Active Monitoring

For small, slow-growing tumors (especially meningiomas and low-grade gliomas), doctors may recommend "watchful waiting" with regular MRI scans to monitor any changes before deciding on treatment.
